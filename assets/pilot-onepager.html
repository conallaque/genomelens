GenomeLens partner pilot

A defined evaluation of whether sequence data can be turned into decisions worth pricing.

GenomeLens is an economic intelligence layer for genome sequencing. This one-page protocol describes a narrow first pilot on a deidentified cohort: what goes in, what comes out, what is measured, and what the pilot deliberately does not attempt to prove.

Objective

Evaluate whether GenomeLens can translate sequencing outputs into traceable, economically qualified decision pathways &mdash; and whether it reliably refuses to value the ones the evidence does not support.

Input

Deidentified whole-genome-derived variant data on a supported build, with a study identifier. Clinical context, laboratory values and medication history are optional and improve qualification where permitted and available.

Output

  • a per-genome economic report
  • a versioned machine-readable partner payload
  • a cohort summary with reconciliation
  • an evidence-gap map naming what would unlock each unvalued pathway

Evaluation questions

  • what share of genomes yield a decision-relevant pathway
  • which categories generate the most pathways
  • how much value is qualified, expected, conditional, or unpriced
  • how much survives comparison with usual care
  • how completely each panel is callable, and where it is not
  • which evidence gaps most often prevent valuation

What success means

  • processing succeeds and failures are reported, not dropped
  • every pathway carries complete economic semantics
  • every valued pathway names a beneficiary and a comparator
  • duplicate-benefit rate is zero
  • unsupported findings produce no invented value
  • the same biology in two file formats produces one answer

Limitations

  • a research and product evaluation, not a clinical study
  • not diagnostic, and not a payer or regulatory submission
  • population-average parameters, not individual predictions
  • no claim of population return on investment
Potential next step

Evaluate GenomeLens on a deidentified sequencing cohort under a predefined pilot protocol, with success measured by traceability, coverage and economic qualification rather than by the size of any dollar figure produced.
